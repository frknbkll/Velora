01 / Start with a design brief

A useful brief answers three questions: who is this for, where will they wear it, and how should it feel? “A relaxed outfit for a creative working day” gives you more direction than “something beautiful.” Choose one setting and three descriptive words before sketching.

Silhouette is the overall outline of a garment. Compare a fitted waist with a straight cut, or a wide leg with a narrow one. Let your brief guide the shape.

02 / Make color and fabric work together

Start with one main color and one supporting neutral. Test them next to each other rather than choosing each in isolation. Contrast can create a focal point; related tones can bring a quieter sense of continuity.

Fabric weight and drape affect how a shape behaves. A structured woven can hold a tailored line; a fluid fabric can soften it. A digital texture is a visual idea, so compare real samples before making or specifying a garment.

03 / Present a clear concept

Give your design a name and explain its purpose in a sentence. Add the silhouette, main color and intended fabric character. A clear concept sketch shows the design direction; it does not replace a technical drawing, measurements or a production pattern.

For a first portfolio page, present one strong design with a short brief and a few considered choices. Show what you intended, then explain how your choices support it.

A few things to know

Before you
begin.

Do I need design experience or special software?

No experience is needed for the introductory workshop. The 3D studio works in browsers with WebGL 2 support; a 2D sketch view is also available. Save GLB models for compatible 3D software, PNG images for your portfolio, or SVG sketches for vector-design tools. These are concept models, not sewing patterns or fabric-fit simulations.

What can I use for free?

The three introductory lessons, design studio, knowledge check and practice certificate are free. The €29, €59 and €99 prices are proposed prices for future courses; paid enrollment and payments are not available yet.

Is the certificate accredited?

No. It is a Velora School certificate of completion for this self-guided introductory workshop. It is not a degree, professional license, accredited qualification or independently verified credential.

Will my progress and designs be saved?

Progress and designs remain in memory during this page visit and reset if you reload or leave. Download your sketch and certificate to keep them. This workshop does not upload your design, answers or certificate name.
